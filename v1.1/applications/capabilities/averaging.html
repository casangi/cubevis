Averaging¶

Status: available. Averaging by a number of integrations has been requested and is not yet available.

What it is¶

Averaging works at three levels:

  1. How samples are averaged:

    • Vector: average the complex visibilities, then take amplitude and phase. This is the default.

    • Scalar: average the amplitudes. Phase uses the circular mean.

  2. What is averaged together:

    • On a raster, each cell combines the samples it covers along the dimensions that are not shown. For example, a Baseline × Time cell averages over channels.

    • On a scatter plot, points can be averaged over time (off, one scan, or a length in seconds) and over channels (off, a number of channels, or all channels in each spectral window).

  3. How baselines are combined, for a raster cell that covers several baselines (any raster without Baseline as an axis):

    • Mean of the baselines’ amplitudes: the general picture. A problem on a few baselines is diluted.

    • Median: what most baselines share, unaffected by a few bad ones.

    • Maximum: interference or a bad antenna shows even if only one baseline has it. A feature that appears with Maximum but not with Median is on only a few baselines.

    • Coherent: the visibilities of all baselines are added before the amplitude is taken. This is meaningful only for a calibrated point source; on anything else the baselines cancel and the plot reads low.

    Vector or scalar averaging still applies to the samples within each baseline.

What it is for¶

  • Vector averaging is a test in itself. Amplitude drops where the samples do not add up: noise, an uncalibrated phase, a delay across the averaged channels. Scalar averaging shows the amplitude whatever the phase does.

  • Scatter averaging gives the standard reduced views: a spectrum per scan (as AIPS POSSM, averaged over time) or a time series (as AIPS VPLOT, averaged over channels).

  • Combining baselines gives one image for many baselines, as in AIPS FTFLG.

How it behaves¶

  • Flagged samples are left out of every average.

  • Time averaging starts at the beginning of each scan and never crosses a scan boundary.

  • A flag box on an averaged plot flags every sample behind the points or cells it encloses.

  • Each plot has its own averaging settings, and the raster’s title names the averaging in use.

  • The windows used by the phase-stability and difference quantities are separate from the averaging. They set what a statistic is measured over, not what a cell covers, so they do not reduce the time resolution of the plot.

How it is calculated¶

Only unflagged samples are averaged, and they are not weighted: the data weights are not used. The vector and scalar averages of one cell or one point are given in How it is calculated.

Several baselines in one raster cell. Each baseline’s samples are first averaged on their own, by vector or scalar averaging. The baselines are then combined:

The other quantities combine baselines as follows:

  • Real and Imaginary are the mean over all samples of all baselines.

  • Amp V Diff and Phase Diff are the mean over all samples. With "max" or "median", each baseline is averaged first and the largest, or the median, baseline is shown.

  • Z-Score is always the largest value in the cell.

  • Phase RMS and Coherence are measured baseline by baseline and then pooled (see How it is calculated).

Scatter averaging. Points are averaged within groups of one baseline, one correlation, one spectral window, one time window and one channel window.

  • Time windows:

    • "scan" gives one window per scan.

    • A number of seconds gives consecutive windows of that length, starting at the beginning of each scan. The last window of a scan may be shorter.

    • A window never spans two scans. Without scan information, a window never spans a gap in time.

  • Channel windows:

    • A number of channels gives blocks of that many channels, counted from the first channel of the spectral window.

    • "all" gives the whole spectral window.

  • The averaged values:

    • Amplitude and Phase are averaged by vector or scalar averaging, as for a raster cell.

    • Real and Imaginary are means.

    • Other Y quantities are not averaged.

  • The point’s X value is the mean of its samples’ X values: mean time, mean frequency, mean UV distance.

  • Other point information: the scan, field and other details used for coloring and the cursor readout are those of the window’s first sample. A window never mixes scans, so they are the same for all its samples.

From Python¶

visplot(vis="obs.ms", averaging="scalar")
visplot(vis="obs.ms", layout="one", kind="scatter", scatter_x="FREQUENCY",
        scatter_y="AMPLITUDE", scatter_avg_time="scan")       # spectrum per scan
visplot(vis="obs.ms", scatter_x="TIME", scatter_avg_chan="all",
        scatter_avg_time=30)                                   # 30 s time series
visplot(vis="obs.ms", raster_y="TIME", raster_x="CHANNEL",
        baseline_combine="max")

Limits¶

  • Averaging by number of integrations is not yet available. Time intervals, both for scatter averaging and for the statistics windows, are given as “off”, “scan” or a length in seconds. AIPS counts in integrations, and since integration times differ between data sets, a count of integrations is often the more natural unit. This has been requested and is planned.

  • Scatter averaging applies to Amplitude, Phase, Real and Imaginary only.

  • Rasters are not averaged in time. A raster with a Time axis shows every integration; AIPS’s smoothing time has no equivalent yet.

  • No spectral smoothing. Channels can be averaged on scatter plots, but not smoothed (AIPS SPFLG’s Gaussian smoothing).
