Rasters, scatter plots and layouts¶

Status: available.

What it is¶

visplot draws two kinds of plot. The same application handles both, so they share one selection, one set of flags and the same conventions:

  • Raster: a 2-D image of a quantity (shown as color) against two of Time, Baseline, Channel, or Antenna 1 × Antenna 2. This is the AIPS TVFLG / SPFLG and CASA msview style of display.

  • Scatter: a quantity against UV distance (in metres or wavelengths), Time, Frequency, Channel, U or V. Plotting U against V gives UV coverage. This is the CASA plotms and AIPS VPLOT / UVPLT style. Points are drawn as density, so millions of samples remain readable.

They can be arranged as:

  • one plot,

  • two plots, side by side or one above the other, each a raster or a scatter with its own settings, or

  • a grid: one plot repeated for each baseline, antenna, spectral window, field, scan or correlation in the selection, page by page, as plotms’s iteraxis does. Two iteration axes can be combined, such as each antenna’s spectral windows in turn.

What it is for¶

A raster shows where a problem is: which baseline, when, at which channel. A scatter shows how much and what shape. Seen side by side, the raster finds a problem and the scatter confirms it, or the other way round. The grid is for comparing many baselines or antennas at a glance.

How it behaves¶

  • Real coordinates. Raster cells are drawn where their coordinates are. A gap between scans or between spectral windows is left blank, not closed up, so rasters and scatter plots of the same data line up.

  • Baseline order. A Baseline axis can be ordered by baseline number, or by length (shortest first). Ordered by length, trouble that grows along the axis is about distance (atmosphere, a resolved source). Trouble in scattered columns is about particular antennas.

  • One correlation per raster. A raster shows one correlation or the mean of the parallel hands. Two rasters on RR and LL show a problem in one hand only.

  • Layers. A scatter can overplot several layers (for example several correlations), each in its own color. A layer can instead be colored by scan, field, antenna, baseline or spectral window, or by Z-Score.

  • Linked axes. Pan and zoom are shared between the panels, and while both plot the same X quantity their X ranges move together. Zooming a raster re-samples the data already read; it does not re-read the data.

  • Time is shown in UTC by default, using the AIPS-style d/hh:mm:ss when the data cross midnight. It can be shown as elapsed time instead.

  • Grid color range. The plots on a page share one color range by default. Alternatively every plot in the grid can share one range, so that pages compare, or each plot can have its own.

  • Grid axes. The plots in a grid can share one axis scale, so that zooming one zooms them all, or each can have its own. Pages can be stepped through, or played in turn.

  • Dark and light themes are available.

How the picture is drawn¶

What is on the screen is not always every cell at full resolution. Knowing how it is reduced matters when looking for a single bad integration.

Very large rasters skip rows and columns.

  • When. A raster has a limit of 2 million cells. Above it, visplot keeps every k-th row and every k-th column, choosing k for each axis so that the result fits and keeps its shape. For example, 351 baselines × 6,000 integrations is 2.1 million cells and would be thinned.

  • What is lost. The skipped rows and columns are not shown at all: they are not averaged into the ones that are kept. On a thinned raster a single bad integration or baseline can be missing from the picture.

  • Zooming. When you zoom in, the visible region is read again at full resolution, but only for Amplitude, Phase, Real, Imaginary and Flag. Z-Score, Phase RMS, Coherence, Amp V Diff and Phase Diff are computed against more than the visible cells (a whole scan or a whole selection), so they stay on the thinned picture even when zoomed.

  • Seeing everything. Narrow the selection (time range, baselines, spectral windows) until the raster is under the limit.

Warning

The plot description page currently reports a thinned raster as “each cell aggregates several samples”. That is not what happens: rows and columns are skipped. This, and the thinning itself, is being changed so that rows are combined rather than skipped.

More cells than screen pixels. When there are more cells than pixels (for example, many integrations across a narrow plot), each pixel shows the mean of the cells it covers. Phase is averaged as a direction. A spike is therefore diluted, not lost, until you zoom in. A pixel whose phases cancel exactly is left blank. When a cell is larger than a pixel, it is drawn with its own value: there is no interpolation between cells, because neighbouring baselines are not related and a gap between scans is not data.

Colors stay put when you zoom. The colors are fixed to the whole raster, not to the region shown. The same value has the same color at every zoom, and zooming in on a quiet region does not stretch its noise across the whole colormap.

Scatter plots are not thinned: every selected sample is drawn.

  • In the default coloring, a pixel’s color is the number of points in it (density).

  • Colored by a category, each category has its own color.

  • A cursor readout reports the values under the pointer.

Axis definitions¶

Axis

Definition

Time

The data’s own time stamps (the integration midpoints), in UTC

Frequency

Each channel’s frequency as stored in the data; there is no frame conversion

Channel

The channel’s index within its spectral window

UV distance (m)

\(\sqrt{u^2 + v^2}\) from the data’s UVW, in metres

UV distance (λ)

UV distance in metres × the channel’s own frequency / c. (A UV range selection in wavelengths uses each spectral window’s centre frequency instead.)

U, V

From the data’s UVW, in metres

Baseline, ordered by length

Ordered by the physical distance between the two antennas’ positions, not the projected baseline length

From Python¶

visplot(vis="obs.ms", layout="side", raster_y="BASELINE", raster_x="TIME",
        raster_qty="AMPLITUDE", scatter_x="UVDIST", scatter_y="AMPLITUDE")

visplot(vis="obs.ms", layout="grid", iterate_by="antenna,spw",
        gridrows=3, gridcols=3, gridcolor="page")

visplot(vis="obs.ms", baseline_order="length", time_labels="elapsed",
        theme="light")

panels=[{...}, {...}] gives each panel’s settings separately.

Limits¶

  • Rasters over 2 million cells skip rows and columns (see How the picture is drawn); narrow the selection to see every cell.

  • At most two independently configured panels; the grid repeats a single plot.

  • Correlation cannot be a raster axis.

  • Elevation, hour angle, azimuth and parallactic angle are not available as axes.
